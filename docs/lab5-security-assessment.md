# NorthStar churn security and privacy assessment

The lab uses synthetic retail data. The current platform stores raw transactions, derived features, model artifacts, and inference capture in a private, versioned S3 bucket with SSE-S3 (AES-256) default encryption and S3 Block Public Access. S3 reported SSE-KMS under the AWS-managed S3 key for sampled SageMaker capture files. Three IAM roles separate ingestion, model work, and monitoring. The deployment used SageMaker production variants for model versions 2 and 3. The assessment covers the controls needed to use this system with real customer data.

## Data classification

The table lists the proposed protection for each asset in a production deployment. SSE-S3 uses keys managed by S3. SSE-KMS with a customer-managed key lets NorthStar control key permissions separately and audit key use. Vendors need access limited to their task, a processing agreement where applicable, and a review of how they will use and transfer the data.

| Asset | Tier | Encryption standard and rationale | IAM access policy | Third-party sharing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer PII | Restricted | SSE-KMS with its own key so decrypt access can be independently restricted and reviewed. | CRM ingestion and case-authorized privacy staff only. Keep model roles outside the PII prefix and key policy. | Only an approved processor with a documented need and named fields. |
| Transaction history | Confidential | SSE-KMS to separately control and audit access to linkable customer purchases. | DataEngineer stages raw and processed transactions. MLEngineer receives approved aggregates only. | Approved processor with minimized fields under contract. |
| Behavioral clickstream | Confidential | SSE-KMS because event sequences can expose individual browsing patterns even under pseudonyms. | Analytics ingestion and approved analysts on scoped prefixes and key grants. | Aggregated or de-identified extracts after privacy review. |
| Product catalog | Public for published fields | SSE-S3 provides baseline at-rest protection for already published catalog attributes. | Catalog publisher writes. Grant public reads from a separate publication path only. | Published product fields only. |
| Model weights | Confidential | SSE-KMS to restrict model IP and track key use for artifact access. | Build role writes versioned artifacts. Serving and approved ModelMonitor workflows read only their needed paths. | Only a vetted hosting processor with scoped access. |
| Inference logs and capture | Restricted | SSE-KMS to constrain decryption of input/output pairs and profiling data. | Serving role writes. Dedicated investigators read approved time ranges. General monitoring uses aggregate metrics. | Approved processor for a specific incident, using redacted extracts where possible. |
| Feature Store records | Confidential | Customer-managed KMS encryption for online storage and SSE-KMS for offline S3. ## Privacy impact assessment

### 1. Personal data inventory

Raw and processed transactions contain the customer join key, purchase dates, category, quantities, channel, payment method, store and spend. Glue uses these records to derive features per customer. The online and offline feature stores include the identifier and timestamps, aggregated behavior, loyalty tier and churn label. Athena exports data used to train and evaluate the ranker. The serving request has 11 numeric features in training order. SageMaker capture records the vector, score, timestamp and request/event metadata, which can be linkable to a person through an upstream request ledger. In a real deployment, treat derived rows and linked predictions as personal data.

### 2. Lawful basis

Choose legitimate interests under GDPR Article 6(1)(f) for scoring purchase history. The purpose is to focus NorthStar's weekly retention budget on customers most likely to disengage. An aggregate purchase-history ranker serves that purpose with fewer identifiers and less behavioral detail than broad profiling. Before launch, document the purpose, why a less intrusive approach would be inadequate, and the balancing test for customer expectations and potential harms. Publish a clear notice, respect objections under Article 21 and stop processing for direct marketing when a person objects. Limit scores to retention eligibility review and verify tier outcomes before outreach. The delivery channel needs its own marketing compliance review. [GDPR Articles 5, 6, 17 and 21](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng/)

### 3. Data minimization

Use aggregates of recency, tenure, frequency, order value, spending, basket size, category diversity and channel mix. Exclude direct identifiers, raw payment details, individual purchase rows and clickstream events from the model input. Reserve the customer ID for joins and the churn label for supervised evaluation and training outcomes. Keep loyalty tier for fairness analysis. Omit the existing heuristic `churn_risk_score` from the feature matrix. Keep request IDs in a separate restricted mapping if operationally necessary. Capture expires from the current view at seven days, then becomes eligible for permanent removal after one day as a prior version, subject to S3 lifecycle processing.

### 4. Right to erasure workflow

Verify the requesting customer and log the scoped case in the privacy system. Pause affected training and refresh jobs during the deletion, and propagate a narrowly scoped suppression marker so the next upstream export cannot recreate the record. Record the legal basis for any mandated retention and restrict those copies to that purpose.

1. **Raw and processed S3.** Find rows where `customer_id = C00123456` in the source exports and derived partitions. Rewrite affected files into a clean generation, reconcile row counts, repoint readers and delete original S3 objects by version ID, including prior versions containing the row. Verify the rewritten tables and check staging files, Athena results, backups and replicas.
2. **Feature Store.** Call `DeleteRecord` with `HardDelete`, target `OnlineStore` and use an event time later than the most recent record. Verify online reads no longer return the record. Offline history uses versioned S3 Parquet in this deployment. Rewrite the affected historical partitions excluding all rows and tombstones for the identifier, repoint the Glue catalog and purge old S3 object versions. Verify with Athena across historical partitions. [AWS Feature Store erasure documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/feature-store-delete-records.html)
3. **Training data and model.** Regenerate training, validation and evaluation manifests from the cleaned source, check the subject is excluded, and purge affected snapshots and cached exports. Review the trained model's privacy exposure. If retraining is required, build and evaluate a clean replacement, approve it, rotate the active endpoint and retire the affected model artifacts and recoverable copies according to the erasure decision.
4. **Inference logs.** Resolve the customer's request IDs through the authorized scoring ledger if one exists. Find matching records in S3 JSONL and any downstream extracts, rewrite affected files, permanently delete their original S3 versions and verify the IDs are gone. Check CloudWatch for application log copies. Capture without identifiers or a request mapping may still be linkable through features. Document the limits of identification and remove the affected capture objects if selective removal cannot be verified. Notify relevant processors and record evidence in the restricted case.

The trained XGBoost model is the hardest step. Individual records influence split selection and leaf values across many trees. There is no row to delete from the serialized model. Retraining requires a clean snapshot, re-running quality and fairness checks, replacing deployed copies, and tracking older artifacts through their retention periods.
